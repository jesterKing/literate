Mapped language diagnostics

This controller owns a separate Problems collection and synchronizes virtual documents without filesystem access. The enabled caller owns resource settings, edit debounce and invalidation; disabling diagnostics must invalidate its session immediately.

An identical snapshot updates freshness without clearing problems or cancelling pulls. Source invalidation clears visible problems and aborts work, but keeps bounded raw reports per client, URI and wire version. After sync, matching reports are remapped to the new source snapshot, including prose-only line shifts; changed wire versions are discarded. Listeners attach before sync; only versioned pushes can be staged. Unversioned pushes are best effort: LSP cannot distinguish a late old report from a current report without a version. They are accepted only while fresh and idle, and cached with the current wire version at receipt, never reused across versions.

Caches keep sanitized ranges and metadata, not server data or source snapshots. Limits are 16 clients, 64 reports per client, 1000 items per report, and 5000 items or 4 MiB of serialized items per client. Oldest reports are evicted; oversized reports are not cached. Client stop (including configuration disposal) purges its cache even after source invalidation; controller disposal purges all caches.

Pull-capable servers take precedence. Requests omit previous result IDs and accept full reports only; unchanged reports and relatedDocuments are ignored. Refreshes coalesce into one sequential, at-most-64-document cycle with no polling or retries.

Only synchronized generated outputs with matching content are owned. Mapping splits code spans, deduplicates expansions, and never invents locations for wrapper text. Messages and metadata are bounded; commands, code-description URLs and related information are not copied, even though relatedInformation support is advertised. Reports retain their independent ownership so clearing one output or stopping one session does not erase another session’s problems.
